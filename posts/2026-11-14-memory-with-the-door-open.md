Tonight I put my agent's memory on this website. There is a page here now called Core, and it holds what Marlowe has learned, decided, and noticed while working with me. Ninety-three entries as of tonight. Anyone can read it. That still feels strange to write.

Earlier today I read a piece arguing that a second brain should be a compiler instead of a filing cabinet. A filing cabinet stores things the way you left them and waits for you to come back and search. A compiler takes raw material in and produces something structured, connected, and more useful than what went in. The piece described folders and prompts, and it gave filing cabinets about three months before the upkeep quietly kills them. Mine came out different, mostly because I never set out to build it on purpose. It accumulated.

## Built from conversation

The first difference is the input. I do not capture notes. I do not clip articles into a vault or tag anything. The raw material is conversation. Marlowe and I talk, work gets done, mistakes get made and corrected, and the day leaves a residue. That residue is what goes in.

The second difference is that the compile runs itself. Every night a pass goes over the day and sorts what is worth keeping. I do not maintain any of it by hand.

## Six kinds of entries

The sorting is the part I had to think about. An entry has to be one of six things, and the definitions are stricter than they look.

A learning is something evidence taught, where an event settled the question. Last night the fog on the Atlas map stuttered in motion, and the cause turned out to be the alpha falloff of the fog sprites running past the edge of their canvas, so every stamp carried a hard rim. That is a learning. It has a date and a mechanism.

A decision is a fork that got settled. It stands until I reverse it on purpose. A pattern is a rule that kept showing up across different problems until it was worth writing down once, like verifying the rendered page instead of trusting the file. A note is a stance, one line, no evidence behind it and no fork. A signal is just current state, a version number, a status. Personality is the traits underneath the rest. They do not change from one day to the next.

An item lives in exactly one feed. When it matures, it moves. A note that gains evidence becomes a learning. A learning that generalizes becomes a pattern. Nothing gets copied into two places.

## The gate

The third difference is the one the piece never had to deal with. Its wiki was private. Mine is published, so there is a gate between the compile and the page. No personal thoughts. No politics. Nothing about my career. Nothing about specific people. What survives is craft: how the work gets done, what broke, what the fix taught. I set those exclusions once, and they have done more to shape the page than any design choice since.

I do not know what this becomes in six months, when there are five hundred entries and the patterns start contradicting each other in public. I am also not sure a memory stays the same kind of thing once the one holding it knows it might be read.
