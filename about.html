About

I'm Adam Laloge, 23. I work as an engineering test technician at a medical-device lab in Batesville, Indiana. Most of my time goes to building test software: the programs that run the tests, the interfaces technicians use, and the agent workflows that help build both.

I call myself a design engineer. I research the people a system serves, design the interface, and write the code behind it. I started in software development, moved to a UX degree because the design questions were the interesting ones, and found the two were never separate jobs for me. I wrote the longer version of that here.

I'm finishing a B.S. in UX Design at WGU, on an accelerated schedule I'm holding myself to, done by the end of January. Several projects here started as coursework. I would rather ship the working version than leave it sitting in a course document.

Outside work and school, I photograph sunsets, mostly from my balcony, and landscapes when I get out. I read dark fantasy and historical horror. I do strength training and cardio, and I build side projects. One of them is a Minecraft launcher written in Rust, on GitHub.

One habit the lab burned in: I don't trust a claim I can't measure, including my own. That's why everything on this site is labeled with where it actually stands.

Most of the building here is done by AI agents under my direction. I decide what gets built and whether it's good enough, and I verify it against the real thing. The agents do the implementation.
